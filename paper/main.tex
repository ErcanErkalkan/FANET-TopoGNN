\begin{abstract}
Rapid motion and correlated radio variation can partition a flying ad hoc network before a
reactive connectivity monitor leaves enough time for intervention. This paper presents
Kinetic-TopoGuard, a short-horizon predictor of the future number of connected components
and of connected-to-fragmented transition risk. The method combines the current graph state,
velocity-projected link margins, zero-dimensional persistence images, graph summaries,
residual regression, and validation-selected risk thresholds. Evaluation separates
sample-level fragmentation classification from one-to-one fragmentation-event precision,
recall, and $F_1$. A run-grouped 20-seed simulation is complemented by a five-seed neural
baseline extension, a six-horizon sweep, a full $2^3$ feature-source factorial, a
load-swept SimPy packet experiment, and complete host-loop latency measurement. Three
real-data layers test different parts of the evidence chain: public field trajectories,
measured UAV-to-UAV \SI{60}{GHz} links with an explicitly transported sensitivity analysis,
and MILUV motion-capture plus inter-robot UWB first-path-power topology. AERPAW cellular
traces increase time-held-out LTE link-state $F_1$ from 0.828/0.679 to 0.961/0.980, while
the temporally robust throughput tests are negative and are reported as such. The
\SI{60}{GHz} link model increases held-out link-viability $F_1$ from 0.731 to 0.923.
At the \SI{0.6}{s} horizon, Kinetic-TopoGuard does not improve count MAE over persistence
(0.133 versus 0.132), but it yields event $F_1=0.483$ with 3.53 false events/minute,
compared with 0.414 and 13.24 for shallow ML. MILUV transfer exposes substantial domain
shift: Kinetic-TopoGuard event $F_1$ is only 0.08 at the primary threshold. The study
supports reproducible short-horizon warning research, but it does not claim a live digital
twin, measured outdoor FANET IP packet delivery, or field deployment.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Fragmentation transitions are scored with one-to-one event matching.
\item Motion, topology, and graph sources are isolated in a full factorial.
\item Twenty seeds and six horizons test the primary forecasting claim.
\item Measured three-UAV UWB topology provides direct multi-UAV transfer evidence.
\item Packet, latency, and physical relay constraints bound operational claims.
\end{highlights}

\section{Introduction}

Flying ad hoc networks (FANETs) support cooperative sensing, mapping, surveillance, and
emergency response without requiring fixed communication infrastructure. Their topology
changes more rapidly than that of terrestrial mobile networks because aircraft move in
three dimensions, execute mission turns, and can separate at tens of metres per second
\cite{BEKMEZCI20131254,pasandideh2023review}. A controller that learns about a partition
only after links disappear is therefore reactive. A useful predictor must estimate
whether the currently connected swarm will fragment at a future control horizon, issue a
warning before the transition, and expose enough structure for an engineer to assess
false-alert cost.

The zeroth Betti number, $\beta_0$, is the number of connected components. It gives a
direct network interpretation: $\beta_0=1$ denotes a connected graph and $\beta_0>1$
denotes fragmentation. Exact graph traversal or disjoint-set algorithms compute
$\beta_0$ for an observed graph \cite{tarjan1975efficiency,cormen2009introduction}, but
they do not forecast its future value. Persistent homology complements a single-radius
graph by recording how components merge as the distance threshold grows. A persistence
image turns those merge scales into a stable fixed-dimensional feature
\cite{adams2017persistence}. In a mobile network, however, topology alone is insufficient:
velocity determines whether a marginal link is closing or opening. This motivates a
motion-conditioned topological forecaster.

Graph neural networks (GNNs) have been applied to routing, clustering, and coverage
\cite{lu2023uav,song2025gnnppor,ye2022multi}, while topology-aware networks combine
message passing with persistent-homology descriptors
\cite{hofer2017deep,carriere2020perslay,luo2023persistent}. These studies do not establish
event-level early warning of connected-to-fragmented FANET transitions. They also commonly
report randomly mixed samples, which can leak neighbouring states from the same flight
into training and testing. The present study instead groups complete runs, freezes
thresholds on validation runs, and distinguishes sample classification from transition
detection.

The accepted predictive-digital-twin literature demonstrates a higher evidence bar than a
stand-alone dashboard: a physical asset, measured sensors, a model, and a validated
information flow must be connected \cite{jasinski2026predictive}. This study does not have
a bidirectional hardware connection and therefore calls its visual interface an
\emph{offline predictive-twin replay}, not a live digital twin. The terminology prevents a
software visualization from being mistaken for deployment evidence.

The research question is:
\emph{Can present motion and multi-scale component structure improve short-horizon
fragmentation event forecasting under run-grouped evaluation, and what operational claims
remain valid when packet, latency, radio-correlation, and real-data checks are added?}

The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A future-horizon formulation that predicts $\beta_0(t+h)$ and separately scores
  connected-to-fragmented events with one-to-one alert matching.
  \item Kinetic-TopoGuard, combining current state, projected link-margin features,
  $H_0$ persistence images, graph summaries, residual count prediction, and calibrated
  risk scoring.
  \item A leakage-controlled evidence suite containing 20 simulation seeds, five-seed
  neural comparisons, six horizons, a full three-source factorial, temporally correlated
  radio, physically bounded relay actions, packet-level simulation, and full-path timing.
  \item External checks that keep their meanings separate: field-motion sensitivity,
  AERPAW UAV-to-infrastructure measurements, held-out \SI{60}{GHz} peer links, and direct
  three-UAV UWB topology from MILUV \cite{shalaby2026miluv}.
  \item A reproducible package whose audit rejects stale caches, missing event metrics,
  unsupported calibration language, incomplete seed grids, and stale PDFs or archives.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}

GCN, GAT, and GraphSAGE learn local representations by aggregating neighbouring node
features \cite{kipf2016semi,velivckovic2018graph,hamilton2017inductive}. FANET and
non-terrestrial-network research has used such encoders for route selection and
reinforcement learning \cite{nemati2022ntn,REN2025103801}. Temporal graph models add
recurrent or continuous-time state, but their performance depends on sequence construction
and run-level separation. None of these architectures makes a future component transition
its primary event target.

Persistent-homology layers expose global structure that shallow message passing can miss.
PH-GNN, PersLay, and related models learn from persistence diagrams or vectorized
topological summaries \cite{hofer2017deep,carriere2020perslay,luo2023persistent}. For
$H_0$, the finite merge times are exactly represented by minimum-spanning-tree edge
lengths. This makes the descriptor computationally attractive for small and medium UAV
swarms. The open issue is not whether persistent homology can be computed, but whether it
adds predictive information after current connectivity and motion are controlled.

External FANET evidence is fragmented across datasets. Public forestry flights provide
real trajectories but no synchronized peer-link labels \cite{araujo2025multidrone}.
AERPAW Datasets 22 and 23 provide aerial cellular KPI and throughput measurements
\cite{aerpawDataset22,aerpawDataset23}. The WiNES \SI{60}{GHz} dataset provides measured
UAV-to-UAV channel observations \cite{winesUavToUav60GHz}. MILUV supplies synchronized
motion capture and UWB measurements for three quadcopters
\cite{shalaby2026miluv}. These sources answer different questions and cannot be merged into
a single claim of field-validated FANET packet delivery.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Physical graph and forecast target}

At time $t$, UAV positions and velocities are
$P_t=\{p_i(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}$ and $V_t=\{v_i(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}$.
A geometric policy first supplies fixed and adaptive candidate radii. Candidate links are
then filtered by a bidirectional received-power rule:
\begin{equation}
(i,j)\in E_t \Longleftrightarrow d_{ij}(t)\le r_t,\quad
P_{i\rightarrow j}(t)\ge\Gamma,\quad P_{j\rightarrow i}(t)\ge\Gamma .
\end{equation}
The link budget uses log-distance path loss, log-normal shadowing, Rayleigh or Nakagami
fading, and transmitter/receiver hardware offsets. Hardware offsets are fixed within a
run. Shadowing and fading evolve as Gauss--Markov processes, so consecutive radio
snapshots are temporally correlated rather than independent draws. The same realization is
used for paired fixed/adaptive-policy comparisons.

For a horizon of $h$ ticks, the count target and sample-level risk label are
\begin{equation}
y_t=\beta_0(G_{t+h}),\qquad z_t=\mathbb{1}[\beta_0(G_{t+h})>1].
\end{equation}
The event target is stricter. An event exists only when
\begin{equation}
\beta_0(G_{t-1})=1\quad\land\quad\beta_0(G_t)>1.
\end{equation}
Thus repeated fragmented samples do not create repeated ground-truth events.

\subsection{Motion-conditioned topology features}

Constant-velocity projection gives
\begin{equation}
\hat p_i(t+h)=p_i(t)+h\Delta t\,v_i(t).
\end{equation}
From current and projected positions, the method extracts pair-distance quantiles,
minimum margins to the active radius, closing/opening rates, projected component counts,
and the number of links expected to cross the radius. Current graph features contain
degree, density, clustering, path, and component summaries.

For $H_0$ persistence, pairwise distances define a Vietoris--Rips filtration. Finite
component deaths are the minimum-spanning-tree merge lengths. Gaussian kernels on birth
and persistence coordinates form a persistence image $\pi_t$. The implementation uses
only $H_0$; it does not claim loop or cavity information.

The complete feature vector is
\begin{equation}
x_t=[c_t,\;g_t,\;\pi_t,\;k_t],
\end{equation}
where $c_t$ is current/lagged component context, $g_t$ is current graph structure,
$\pi_t$ is the persistence image, and $k_t$ contains kinematic projections.

\subsection{Residual count and risk models}

Kinetic-TopoGuard anchors count prediction at the observed component count:
\begin{equation}
\hat y_t=\max\{1,\;\beta_0(G_t)+\alpha f_{\theta}(x_t)\}.
\end{equation}
An ExtraTrees regressor estimates the residual. The scale $\alpha$ is selected on
validation runs to minimize MAE and prevents unstable corrections from dominating the
physical anchor. A separate balanced ExtraTrees classifier gives
$s_t=P(z_t=1\mid x_t)$. The alert threshold is selected on validation data only.

For each ground-truth transition, at most one preceding alert in $[t-h,t-1]$ is matched.
An alert cannot match multiple events and alerts issued while the graph is already
fragmented are ignored. The study reports event precision, recall, $F_1$, missed events,
and unmatched false-alert events per minute. Sample-level $F_1$, PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, Brier
score, and calibration error remain complementary diagnostics.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Kinetic-TopoGuard inference and alerting}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $P_t,V_t,G_t$, horizon $h$, fitted $f_\theta,q_\phi$, threshold $\tau$
\State Project positions to $\hat P_{t+h}$
\State Compute current graph, kinematic, and $H_0$ persistence features
\State $\hat y_t\gets\max(1,\beta_0(G_t)+\alpha f_\theta(x_t))$
\State $s_t\gets q_\phi(x_t)$
\State Emit an alert iff $\beta_0(G_t)=1$ and $s_t\ge\tau$
\State \Return $\hat y_t,s_t$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Controller boundary}

The forecast can trigger one-step relay steering, route recomputation, or buffering.
Relay motion is constrained by current velocity, $\Delta t$, maximum speed
\SI{30}{m.s^{-1}}, and maximum acceleration \SI{12}{m.s^{-2}}. A relay action receives at
most a \SI{3}{dB} incident-link budget improvement; it does not expand every network edge.
The analytical controller output is called a \emph{reachability-delivery proxy}, not PDR.
Actual simulated PDR and delay are reported only by the separate SimPy packet experiment.
The controller is a counterfactual one-step test and not a flight-control implementation.

\section{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Simulation and leakage control}

Mission trajectories combine scan, transit, and loiter phases. Swarm sizes, mobility
families, fixed/adaptive graph policies, and nominal/degraded radio scenarios are crossed
within each seed. Complete base runs, identified by mobility, swarm size, and seed, are
assigned to train, validation, or test. Policy and radio variants of one trajectory remain
in the same split. No test sample selects a learner, residual scale, risk threshold, or
alert budget.

The primary confirmation uses 20 independent seeds and compares Kinetic-TopoGuard,
validation-selected shallow ML, and current-state persistence. Persistence predicts
$\beta_0(t+h)=\beta_0(t)$ and is a deployable no-change baseline. The neural extension
uses five seeds for GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, PI+MLP, two FANET-TopoGNN variants, T-GCN,
STGCN, and TGN. Backend records must identify PyTorch; surrogate neural rows are rejected.

MAE and $R^2$ confidence intervals use clustered bootstrap resampling of complete runs.
Risk metrics are computed from held-out samples. Event metrics use the transition matching
defined in Section~\ref{sec:method}. The two operating policies impose validation budgets
of two and one false alert events per minute. Feasibility is recorded rather than assumed.

\subsection{Stress, ablation, packet, and latency studies}

The horizon study reuses each physical trajectory and radio realization and deterministically
reindexes labels at 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 1.0, 1.5, and \SI{2.0}{s}. Five seeds are used at every
horizon. The feature study crosses graph, topology, and kinematic sources in a complete
$2^3$ factorial. Every row uses the same ExtraTrees learner and current-state context,
preventing a stronger classifier from being attributed to one feature family.

The SimPy experiment evaluates 8, 16, 32, and 64 offered packets per \SI{0.1}{s} tick.
Models receive identical source--destination demand and random seeds. It implements finite
queues, route availability, CSMA-style random backoff, a single collision domain, and a
per-tick deadline. This is a controlled packet abstraction, not ns-3, OMNeT++, or a field
MAC trace.

Host-loop timing starts before pairwise-distance construction. It includes fixed/adaptive
physical-radio graphs, graph statistics, persistence images, feature assembly, count
prediction, and warning score. Five seeds and evenly spread test snapshots are timed.
Sensor acquisition, operating-system transport, and autopilot actuation are excluded.

\subsection{External evidence layers}

\textbf{Forestry motion.} Three synchronized public flight trajectories are sampled at
\SI{10}{Hz}. Because the bags contain no peer RF or packet labels, communication radii are
predeclared counterfactual sensitivities. Models and thresholds are frozen from simulation.

\textbf{AERPAW cellular.} LTE connection state is predicted with a chronological final-30\%
test split and compared with a validation-derived RSRP threshold. iPerf throughput uses
both final-30\%-by-time and first-half-to-second-half splits. The lag-1 throughput
autocorrelation is reported, and interleaved random blocks are prohibited.

\textbf{\SI{60}{GHz} UAV-to-UAV channel.} A measured peer-link model is trained below
\SI{33}{m} and tested on held-out distances at or above \SI{33}{m}. Link viability uses
the same SNR and received-power rule in model testing and downstream sensitivity.
Applying the measured distance-success relationship to the forestry motion is a
\emph{transported RF sensitivity}, not same-site calibration. Primary summaries require
all three pair distances to remain inside the measured 6--\SI{40}{m} support; clamped
full-trace values are secondary.

\textbf{MILUV UWB topology.} The official Figshare archive for
\path{cirObstacles_3_random_0} is accessed by HTTP range extraction. Only six Vicon
and UWB CSV members are retained, with archive CRC and SHA-256 verification. Motion is
interpolated at \SI{10}{Hz}; reciprocal ranging records are deduplicated; and each
robot-pair edge uses the latest causal first-path-power observation no older than
\SI{6}{s}. The longest interval with all three pair states known is evaluated at
$-90$, $-92$, and \SI{-95}{dBm}. No MILUV sample is used for fitting or threshold
selection; the three-threshold sensitivity grid is fixed before model evaluation and is
not optimized against MILUV outcomes. These labels describe measured UWB ranging quality,
not IP PDR.

events/minute; the one-event policy trades recall for precision, yielding event
$F_1=0.351$ at 0.90 false events/minute.

\subsection{Comparison with a predictive digital-twin acceptance bar}

The bridge-monitoring case study of Jasinski et al.\ integrates a physical asset, measured
sensors, a model-based representation, machine learning, and a validated information flow
\cite{jasinski2026predictive}. The present work now matches several methodological
strengths: explicit system architecture, measured-data checks, reproducible model
evaluation, and an engineering decision layer. It does not match the physical integration
bar. Its replay is generated from completed experiments and sends no command to a UAV.
Calling it an offline replay preserves the distinction between predictive analytics and a
bidirectional operational twin.

\section{Limitations}

Four boundaries remain. First, the primary training domain is simulated even though radio
errors are temporally correlated and hardware offsets persist within runs. Second, MILUV
is indoor UWB ranging quality and contains only three UAVs; it is not outdoor FANET IP
traffic. Third, the SimPy model has a single collision domain and simplified routing,
queues, and deadlines. Fourth, the relay response is a one-step counterfactual with speed,
acceleration, and \SI{3}{dB} link-budget limits, not a closed-loop flight experiment.

Consequently, no result establishes field PDR, energy use, regulatory feasibility,
multi-channel interference, or safe autonomous actuation. The next confirmatory study
requires synchronized outdoor peer-to-peer packet traces, onboard timing and power
measurement, and a preregistered closed-loop intervention protocol. These are limitations,
not claims closed by prose.

\section{Reproducibility}

The anonymous package contains configurations, source code, per-seed outputs, protocols,
derived real-data traces, hashes, generated tables, and figures. The MILUV downloader
records the official dataset DOI, archive identifier, archive MD5, member CRC values, and
per-file SHA-256 values. A readiness audit checks cache version, seed coverage, PyTorch
backend provenance, event columns, validation/test separation, horizon and factorial
grids, packet loads, latency stages, support-aware \SI{60}{GHz} scope, MILUV transfer,
PDF page counts, anonymization, ZIP CRC, and package freshness.

The included dashboard is titled ``Offline FANET Predictive-Twin Replay.'' It binds only
to generated CSV/JSON evidence, is interactive for operating-policy comparison, and
contains an explicit notice that it is not live, bidirectional, or hardware connected.
This interface is a reproducibility aid rather than an independent experiment.

\section{Conclusion}

Kinetic-TopoGuard formulates FANET monitoring as future component-count and
fragmentation-event forecasting. The revised evaluation replaces ambiguous topology-change
alerts with connected-to-fragmented events, uses one-to-one matching, selects thresholds
only on validation runs, and adds temporal radio, physical relay limits, factorial,
horizon, packet, latency, and real multi-UAV UWB checks. The evidence supports a
reproducible short-horizon warning method, but it also shows where claims fail: topological
features do not dominate every objective, AERPAW throughput transfer is not robust, and
better warning scores do not guarantee higher packet delivery. These negative results
define the appropriate boundary for follow-on hardware validation.
