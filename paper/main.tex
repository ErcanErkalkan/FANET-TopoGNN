\begin{abstract}
Rapid motion and correlated radio variation can fragment an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV)
network before reactive monitoring leaves time for a safe response. We test whether
short-horizon warnings remain useful when false alerts, packet traffic, controller cost,
domain shift, and computation are considered jointly. The artificial-intelligence
contribution is a leakage-controlled benchmark of current-state, topological, kinematic,
source-gated, shallow, and neural predictors with a locked 20-seed evaluation. The
engineering application couples validation-selected warnings to bounded simulated relay
and packet-event models; it is not field control. Under the prespecified
multiplicity-controlled rule, Source-Gated Kinetic-TopoGuard does not outperform
Current-state ExtraTrees: the paired event F1 difference is -0.0215 (seed-bootstrap 95\%
confidence interval [-0.0417, -0.0020]; Holm-adjusted permutation p = 0.1030), while false
alerts increase by 2.10 events per minute. In the 20-seed equal-learner factorial, adding
graph, topology, and kinematic sources reduces event F1 by 0.0426, although calibration
improves. The residual count branch selects zero scale in 95\% of seeds, and none of 360
paired closed-loop comparisons satisfies the predeclared engineering-benefit rule. The
single compatible measured three-vehicle ultra-wideband sequence contains only one or two
fragmentation events per threshold; all frozen and adapted models yield zero event F1 at
the primary threshold. We treat this as a sparse stress test, not strong external
validation. Measured host-loop latency excludes sensing, telemetry, autopilot
communication, and actuation. The benchmark exposes where richer predictors fail to yield
engineering benefit.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item The locked primary test finds no supported source-gated superiority.
\item A full factorial isolates current, graph, topology, and motion sources.
\item Packet and controller experiments expose prediction-to-action limits.
\item Cross-domain tests reveal severe fragmentation-event transfer failure.
\item Runtime measurements bound claims to the host-side algorithm loop.
\end{highlights}

\section{Introduction}

Flying ad hoc networks (FANETs) support cooperative sensing, mapping, surveillance, and
emergency response without requiring fixed communication infrastructure. Their topology
changes more rapidly than that of terrestrial mobile networks because aircraft move in
three dimensions, execute mission turns, and can separate at tens of metres per second
\cite{BEKMEZCI20131254,pasandideh2023review}. A controller that learns about a partition
only after links disappear is therefore reactive. A useful predictor must estimate
whether the currently connected swarm will fragment at a future control horizon, issue a
warning before the transition, and expose enough structure for an engineer to assess
false-alert cost.

The zeroth Betti number, $\beta_0$, is the number of connected components. It gives a
direct network interpretation: $\beta_0=1$ denotes a connected graph and $\beta_0>1$
denotes fragmentation. Exact graph traversal or disjoint-set algorithms compute
$\beta_0$ for an observed graph \cite{tarjan1975efficiency,cormen2009introduction}, but
they do not forecast its future value. Persistent homology complements a single-radius
graph by recording how components merge as the distance threshold grows. A persistence
image turns those merge scales into a stable fixed-dimensional feature
\cite{adams2017persistence}. In a mobile network, however, velocity determines whether a
marginal link is closing or opening. The engineering question is not merely whether these
descriptors can be computed, but whether they add reliable decision value beyond a strong
current-state comparator.

Graph neural networks (GNNs) have been applied to routing, clustering, and coverage
\cite{lu2023uav,song2025gnnppor,ye2022multi}, while topology-aware networks combine
message passing with persistent-homology descriptors
\cite{hofer2017deep,carriere2020perslay}. These studies do not establish
event-level early warning under matched learners, unused-seed confirmation, packet-level
control, and cross-domain transfer. Randomly mixed samples can also leak neighbouring states
from one flight into training and testing. The gap is therefore an evidence problem: model
families, feature sources, operating thresholds, and engineering outcomes must be compared
without letting held-out tests select the story.

The study addresses three research questions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Under run-grouped and locked-seed evaluation, do graph, topological, kinematic, or
  source-gated predictors improve fragmentation-event $F_1$ over Current-state ExtraTrees?
  \item Do improvements in forecast or calibration metrics survive chronological public-data
  transfer and produce paired packet/controller benefit at acceptable action cost?
  \item Which computational and evidence boundaries prevent host-side simulation results from
  being interpreted as onboard, field, or deployment-ready performance?
\end{enumerate}

The contributions follow the benchmark/stress-test route selected from the completed
evidence:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Evaluation formulation:} future fragmentation risk is separated from
  component-count regression, and one-to-one event matching prevents prolonged fragmentation
  or duplicate warnings from inflating performance.
  \item \textbf{Artificial-intelligence benchmark:} current-state, graph,
  persistent-homology, kinematic, source-gated, shallow, and neural model families are
  compared with run-grouped splits, a 20-seed equal-learner factorial, and a locked 20-seed
  test unused in development.
  \item \textbf{Engineering application:} validation-selected warnings drive a causal,
  physically bounded relay policy whose graph outcomes are kept separate from packet-event
  delivery, delay, drop reasons, and action cost.
  \item \textbf{Cross-domain stress test:} field motion, UAV-to-infrastructure cellular
  measurements, held-out peer-link measurements, and three-UAV ultra-wideband topology are
  assigned explicit and non-overlapping claim boundaries.
  \item \textbf{Reproducible negative evidence:} source-induced event degradation, an
  inactive residual branch, weak measured-topology transfer, absent closed-loop support, and
  model-dependent host latency are retained rather than selected away.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}

\textbf{Future fragmentation and link forecasting.} GCN, GAT, and GraphSAGE aggregate
neighbouring node state \cite{kipf2017semi,velickovic2018graph,hamilton2017inductive};
FANET and non-terrestrial-network studies mainly apply such encoders to routing,
clustering, or policy learning \cite{lu2023uav,song2025gnnppor,ye2022multi,nemati2022ntn,REN2025103801}.
Recent UAV link prediction combines multiscale attention with recurrent temporal state
\cite{pu2026must}. Those tasks predict links or actions, whereas this study predicts the
first future transition from one connected component to multiple components. That target
requires a system-level comparator: a link predictor can improve edge accuracy without
improving the timing or false-alert burden of a fragmentation warning.

\textbf{Temporal and topology-aware learning.} Recurrent graph convolution, STGCN, and
continuous-time TGN provide distinct temporal inductive biases
\cite{zhao2020tgcn,yu2018stgcn,rossi2020tgn}. PH-GNN, PersLay, and persistence images expose
global multiscale structure that shallow message passing can miss
\cite{hofer2017deep,carriere2020perslay,adams2017persistence}. For $H_0$, finite merge times
are minimum-spanning-tree edge lengths, making the descriptor tractable for UAV swarms.
The unresolved task-specific question is whether these sources add held-out event value
after recent component state and kinematics are controlled with the same learner. The
equal-learner factorial and the contextual neural extension are designed around that
comparison rather than an architecture-only leaderboard.

\textbf{Leakage-safe event evaluation.} Neighbouring snapshots from one flight are strongly
dependent, so random row splits can expose a model to the same trajectory in training and
test. The present protocol groups complete runs and freezes validation-selected thresholds.
It also adapts range-aware time-series detection principles \cite{tatbul2018precision} to a
one-to-one, pre-transition matching rule. This distinction matters because sample $F_1$
can reward prolonged fragmented periods, while the engineering endpoint concerns one early
warning per transition and the number of unmatched warnings per minute.

\textbf{From prediction to engineering evidence.} Public sources observe different links
in the causal chain: forestry flights provide motion without peer-radio labels
\cite{araujo2025multidrone}; AERPAW provides aerial cellular KPI and throughput
\cite{aerpawDataset22,aerpawDataset23}; WiNES provides measured UAV-to-UAV channel samples
\cite{winesUavToUav60GHz,polese2020experimental}; and MILUV combines indoor motion capture
with three-robot UWB measurements \cite{shalaby2026miluv,miluvDataset}. None alone validates
outdoor FANET warning-to-packet-to-actuation performance. Accordingly, this work separates
forecast metrics, measured-data transfer, packet-event simulation, action cost, and
host-loop latency instead of treating any one layer as deployment evidence.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Physical graph and forecast target}

At time $t$, UAV positions and velocities are
$P_t=\{p_i(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}$ and $V_t=\{v_i(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}$.
A geometric policy first supplies fixed and adaptive candidate radii. Candidate links are
then filtered by a bidirectional received-power rule:
\begin{equation}
(i,j)\in E_t \Longleftrightarrow d_{ij}(t)\le r_t,\quad
P_{i\rightarrow j}(t)\ge\Gamma,\quad P_{j\rightarrow i}(t)\ge\Gamma .
\end{equation}
The link budget uses log-distance path loss, log-normal shadowing, Rayleigh or Nakagami
fading, and transmitter/receiver hardware offsets, following standard radio abstractions
and the established correlated-shadowing and Nakagami models
\cite{goldsmith2005wireless,gudmundson1991correlation,nakagami1960mdistribution}. Hardware offsets are fixed within a
run. Shadowing and fading evolve as Gauss--Markov processes, so consecutive radio
snapshots are temporally correlated rather than independent draws. The same realization is
used for paired fixed/adaptive-policy comparisons.

Table~\ref{tab:radio-control-parameters} makes the physical and control assumptions
auditable. Literature-backed model forms are distinguished from study-specific stress
settings; none of the numerical settings is presented as a calibrated radio, certified
airframe, or regulatory limit.

For a horizon of $h$ ticks, the count target and sample-level risk label are
\begin{equation}
y_t=\beta_0(G_{t+h}),\qquad z_t=\mathbb{1}[\beta_0(G_{t+h})>1].
\end{equation}
The event target is stricter. An event exists only when
\begin{equation}
\beta_0(G_{t-1})=1\quad\land\quad\beta_0(G_t)>1.
\end{equation}
Thus repeated fragmented samples do not create repeated ground-truth events.

\subsection{Motion-conditioned topology features}

Constant-velocity projection gives
\begin{equation}
\hat p_i(t+h)=p_i(t)+h\Delta t\,v_i(t).
\end{equation}
From current and projected positions, the method extracts pair-distance quantiles,
minimum margins to the active radius, closing/opening rates, projected component counts,
and the number of links expected to cross the radius. Current graph features contain
degree, density, clustering, path, and component summaries.

For $H_0$ persistence, pairwise distances define a Vietoris--Rips filtration. Finite
component deaths are the minimum-spanning-tree merge lengths. Gaussian kernels on birth
and persistence coordinates form a persistence image $\pi_t$. The implementation uses
only $H_0$; it does not claim loop or cavity information.

The complete feature vector is
\begin{equation}
x_t=[c_t,\;g_t,\;\pi_t,\;k_t],
\end{equation}
where $c_t$ is current/lagged component context, $g_t$ is current graph structure,
$\pi_t$ is the persistence image, and $k_t$ contains kinematic projections.

\subsection{Comparators, residual prediction, and risk scoring}

Two current-state references have different roles. \emph{Current-state persistence} is
deterministic: it sets $\hat y_t=\beta_0(G_t)$ and does not fit a learner. It must not be
confused with \emph{Current-state ExtraTrees}, which fits an ExtraTrees residual regressor
and a balanced ExtraTrees risk classifier from current and lagged component state, component
change, node count, and active radius. The latter uses the same run split, validation
threshold rule, and test evaluation as the richer equal-learner factorial rows.
The fitted implementation uses the original Extremely Randomized Trees method through
scikit-learn \cite{geurts2006extremely,pedregosa2011scikit}.

Kinetic-TopoGuard anchors count prediction at the observed component count:
\begin{equation}
\hat y_t=\max\{1,\;\beta_0(G_t)+\alpha f_{\theta}(x_t)\}.
\end{equation}
An ExtraTrees regressor estimates the residual. The scale $\alpha$ is selected from a grid
that includes zero, using validation mean absolute error (MAE) only; the test partition
cannot influence whether the residual branch is active. A separate balanced ExtraTrees
classifier gives $s_t=P(z_t=1\mid x_t)$. Classifier choice, score blending, and the alert
threshold are selected on validation runs only.

\subsection{Source-Gated Kinetic-TopoGuard}

Source-Gated Kinetic-TopoGuard tests whether learned source selection is safer than forced
feature concatenation. Four base sources are used: current state, graph summaries,
zero-dimensional persistence features, and kinematic projections. Within the training
partition, GroupKFold cross-fitting holds out complete split groups. Every training row
therefore receives count-residual and fragmentation-risk predictions from base learners
that were not fitted on that row, and no run identifier appears on both sides of one fold.
The four out-of-fold count predictions train a ridge meta-regressor; the four out-of-fold
risk scores train a regularized logistic meta-classifier. Base learners are then refitted
on the full training partition for inference.

Development validation runs select minimum leaf sizes, ridge regularization, logistic
regularization, probability calibration from none, sigmoid, or isotonic regression, and the
event threshold. Calibration is evaluated with reliability diagnostics, Brier score, and
expected calibration error \cite{brier1950verification,guo2017calibration}. Threshold selection maximizes validation event $F_1$, then sample $F_1$,
then minimizes false events per minute, and finally prefers the higher threshold. The test
partition is evaluated once after these choices. Stored artefacts contain base parameters,
meta coefficients, calibration type, threshold, feature-source names, and cross-fitting
run identifiers. For the locked confirmation, the modal validation-selected development
package is frozen before any new-seed test result is observed.

For each ground-truth transition at index $t$, eligible alerts are those emitted while the
graph is connected in $[t-h,t-1]$. Events and alerts are processed chronologically; one
eligible alert can match at most one event, one event can receive at most one match, and
alerts emitted after fragmentation begins are ineligible. Unmatched eligible alerts are
false-alert events. The study reports event precision, recall, $F_1$, missed events, and
false-alert events per simulated minute. This explicit one-to-one rule is study-specific;
range-aware time-series precision and recall provide the broader evaluation context
\cite{tatbul2018precision}. Sample-level $F_1$, precision--recall area,
receiver-operating-characteristic area, Brier score, and expected calibration error remain
complementary diagnostics rather than substitutes for the event endpoint.

\paragraph{Inference sequence.} Given $P_t,V_t,G_t$, horizon $h$, fitted
$f_\theta,q_\phi$, and threshold $\tau$: (1) project positions to $\hat P_{t+h}$;
(2) compute current-graph, kinematic, and $H_0$ persistence features; (3) calculate
$\hat y_t=\max(1,\beta_0(G_t)+\alpha f_\theta(x_t))$ and $s_t=q_\phi(x_t)$; and
(4) emit an alert only if $\beta_0(G_t)=1$ and $s_t\ge\tau$.

\subsection{Controller and packet-simulation boundary}

The analytical one-step controller tests graph reachability after a bounded relay change;
its output is a \emph{reachability-delivery proxy}, not packet delivery ratio. The separate
stateful closed-loop experiment carries relay position, velocity, reaction delay, action
duration, and cooldown across controller ticks. Relay motion has maximum speed
\SI{30}{m.s^{-1}} and maximum acceleration \SI{12}{m.s^{-2}}, and only relay-incident links receive at most a
\SI{3}{dB} budget improvement. This bounded repositioning study is related to aerial-relay
connectivity maintenance \cite{yanmaz2022positioning}. The coupled SimPy discrete-event
model \cite{simpy412} separately generates packet
arrivals, queues, routes, serialization, deadlines, retransmission outcomes, and explicit
drop causes. Only delivery events from that packet simulator are called packet delivery
ratio. Neither controller is a flight-control implementation.

\section{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Simulation and leakage control}

Mission trajectories combine scan, transit, and loiter phases. Swarm sizes, mobility
families, fixed/adaptive graph policies, and nominal/degraded radio scenarios are crossed
within each seed. Complete base runs, identified by mobility, swarm size, and seed, are
assigned to train, validation, or test. Policy and radio variants of one trajectory remain
in the same split. No test sample selects a learner, residual scale, risk threshold, or
alert budget. Training, validation, and test run-identifier intersections are checked as
empty for every seed. All paired models within a seed share the same mobility and radio
realization.

The original 20-seed benchmark compares Kinetic-TopoGuard, validation-selected shallow
machine learning, and deterministic current-state persistence. The equal-learner factorial
adds Current-state ExtraTrees and all eight combinations of graph, topology, and kinematic
sources. The five-seed model-family extension contains the same five non-neural references
and nine PyTorch baselines: graph convolutional, graph attention, GraphSAGE,
persistence-image multilayer perceptron, two FANET-TopoGNN variants, temporal graph
convolutional, spatio-temporal graph convolutional, and temporal graph network models.
They use PyTorch \cite{paszke2019pytorch}; the repository also exposes an optional
PyTorch Geometric adapter \cite{fey2019fast}, although PyTorch Geometric was not installed
in the measured five-seed runtime. Every neural aggregate requires the same five seeds and a verified PyTorch backend;
surrogate or incomplete rows are rejected. Because these neural rows have only five seeds,
they are descriptive model-family context: they are not included in the locked hypothesis,
do not support superiority claims, and are not pooled with any 20-seed aggregate. The full
Supplementary Material S1 table reports all 14 models, parameter counts where defined,
training budgets, event metrics, and seed coverage. A separately documented 20-new-seed
extension is pending execution on a verified PyTorch backend; this manuscript reports no
result for that unexecuted protocol.

After the five-seed Source-Gated development study, a separate confirmation is locked by
SHA-256 hash. Its 20 seeds are generated by a domain-separated SHA-256 counter stream and
are rejected if they appear in any prior configuration or output. Before those test
outcomes are read, the feature definitions, horizon, base and meta hyperparameters,
isotonic calibration, threshold grid and tie-break, primary comparator, primary event-$F_1$
hypothesis, and evaluation models are frozen. The lock includes the config hash, source
artefact hashes, excluded prior seeds, and git commit. A hash mismatch aborts execution;
each test partition is evaluated once.

MAE and $R^2$ confidence intervals use clustered bootstrap resampling of complete runs.
Risk metrics are computed from held-out samples. Event metrics use the transition matching
defined in Section~\ref{sec:method}. The two operating policies impose validation budgets
of two and one false alert events per minute. Feasibility is recorded rather than assumed.
For the locked primary event-$F_1$ comparison, the seed-bootstrap interval is reported as
an effect-size uncertainty summary, while the paired permutation test supplies the
confirmatory $p$-value. The prespecified claim requires a positive paired difference, a
bootstrap interval excluding zero, and Holm-adjusted permutation $p<0.05$ across the locked
primary comparisons. When these summaries differ, the multiplicity-controlled rule governs
the superiority decision; no single unadjusted or interval result is used to override it.

\subsection{Stress, ablation, packet, and latency studies}

The horizon study reuses each physical trajectory and radio realization and deterministically
reindexes labels at 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 1.0, 1.5, and \SI{2.0}{s}. Five seeds are used at every
horizon. The feature study crosses graph, topology, and kinematic sources in a complete
$2^3$ factorial. Every row uses the same ExtraTrees learner and current-state context,
preventing a stronger classifier from being attributed to one feature family.

The event-definition sensitivity keeps the fitted Kinetic-TopoGuard score and its
validation-selected threshold fixed. On held-out runs it varies the pre-transition warning
window and refractory period over 0.3, 0.6, and \SI{1.2}{s}, and reports results across
within-seed low, middle, and high measured event-density tertiles. These analyses test the
stability of event accounting; they do not retune the model or replace the locked endpoint.

The packet-load sweep evaluates 8, 16, 32, and 64 offered packets per \SI{0.1}{s} tick.
Models receive identical source--destination demand and random seeds. It implements finite
queues, route availability, CSMA-style random backoff, a single collision domain, and a
per-tick deadline. This is a controlled packet abstraction, not ns-3, OMNeT++, or a field
MAC trace.

The stateful closed-loop study instead crosses three packet loads and swarm sizes of 10,
20, and 30 for six policies. Mobility, radio, packet arrivals, and packet random seeds are
paired. A policy reads only current/past state and its current risk score; thresholds and
action parameters are fixed from validation or a priori limits. Engineering benefit
requires a packet-delivery-ratio confidence interval excluding zero, Holm-adjusted paired
$p<0.05$, relay travel below \SI{300}{m} per simulated minute, and action duty cycle below
0.25.

Host-loop timing separates pairwise distance, radio graph, graph statistics, persistence
image, feature assembly, count inference, risk inference, calibration, and controller
decision. After five warm-ups, 30 iterations are measured at 10, 20, 30, and 40 UAVs under
single- and default-thread modes. Sensor acquisition, telemetry, scheduling outside the
process, autopilot communication, actuation, and network transfer are excluded.

\subsection{External evidence layers}

\textbf{Forestry motion.} Three synchronized public flight trajectories are sampled at
\SI{10}{Hz}. Because the bags contain no peer RF or packet labels, communication radii are
predeclared counterfactual sensitivities. Models and thresholds are frozen from simulation.

\textbf{AERPAW cellular.} LTE connection state is predicted with a chronological final-30\%
test split and compared with a validation-derived RSRP threshold. iPerf throughput uses
both final-30\%-by-time and first-half-to-second-half splits. The lag-1 throughput
autocorrelation is reported, and interleaved random blocks are prohibited.

\textbf{\SI{60}{GHz} UAV-to-UAV channel.} A measured peer-link model is trained below
\SI{33}{m} and tested on held-out distances at or above \SI{33}{m}. Link viability uses
the same SNR and received-power rule in model testing and downstream sensitivity.
Applying the measured distance-success relationship to the forestry motion is a
\emph{transported RF sensitivity}, not same-site calibration. Primary summaries require
all three pair distances to remain inside the measured 6--\SI{40}{m} support; clamped
full-trace values are secondary.

\textbf{MILUV UWB topology.} The official Figshare archive for
\path{cirObstacles_3_random_0} is accessed by HTTP range extraction. Only six Vicon
and UWB CSV members are retained, with archive CRC and SHA-256 verification. Motion is
interpolated at \SI{10}{Hz}; reciprocal ranging records are deduplicated; and each
robot-pair edge uses the latest causal first-path-power observation no older than
\SI{6}{s}. The longest interval with all three pair states known is evaluated at
$-90$, $-92$, and \SI{-95}{dBm}. No MILUV sample is used for fitting or threshold
selection; the three-threshold sensitivity grid is fixed before model evaluation and is
not optimized against MILUV outcomes. These labels describe measured UWB ranging quality,
not IP PDR. The evaluated sequence contains two connected-to-fragmented events at
$-90$ and $-92$ dBm and one at $-95$ dBm. This event support is sufficient only for a
sparse failure-oriented stress test, not for a precise external-validation estimate.

The frozen zero-shot MILUV result remains the primary transfer evidence. A secondary
chronological study uses an early calibration prefix, a guard interval, and a later test
segment. Base weights remain frozen for probability calibration and threshold selection;
a separately labelled few-shot protocol may update only its predeclared prediction head
from the prefix. Adjacent samples are never randomly mixed. Leave-one-scenario-out testing
is reported unavailable because only one compatible provenance-verified three-robot
sequence is present.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Locked primary confirmation and benchmark context}

The locked comparison is reported first because it is the only evaluation whose 20 seeds
were excluded from all prior configurations and outputs. Table~\ref{tab:locked-confirmatory}
shows the complete locked aggregate. Current-state ExtraTrees obtains event $F_1=0.498$,
whereas Source-Gated Kinetic-TopoGuard obtains 0.477. The paired Source-Gated-minus-current
difference is $-0.0215$ (seed-bootstrap 95\% CI [$-0.0417$,$-0.0020$]); the paired
permutation $p$-value is 0.0515 and its Holm-adjusted value is 0.1030. The predeclared
superiority rule is therefore not met. The interval summarizes the observed paired
effect, whereas the Holm-adjusted permutation result controls the confirmatory decision;
the apparently different thresholds are not treated as interchangeable evidence. Event
precision decreases by 0.0831 and false alerts
increase by 2.10 events/minute (95\% CI [1.12, 3.07], Holm-adjusted $p=0.00249$).
Although the Source-Gated model improves Brier score and expected calibration error, those
secondary gains do not overturn the negative primary event decision.

The earlier 20-seed benchmark remains useful as protocol context rather than the primary
superiority test; its complete table is in Supplementary Material S1. Event $F_1$ penalizes duplicate and
unmatched alerts and exposes missed transitions. Deterministic persistence has the lowest
count MAE (0.132 versus 0.133 for Kinetic-TopoGuard), while Kinetic-TopoGuard raises event
$F_1$ from 0.414 for shallow machine learning to 0.483 and reduces false alerts from 13.24
to 3.53 per minute. This is a warning-trade-off result against the shallow comparator, not
evidence that the residual count branch or richer sources outperform current-state models.

The five-seed neural extension is retained as model-family context rather than folded into
the 20-seed claim. This distinction avoids implying that every expensive neural baseline
was run for 20 seeds. Kinetic-TopoGuard obtains MAE 0.120 and event $F_1=0.470$.
STGCN has the lowest neural MAE (0.218), while TGN has the highest neural event
$F_1$ (0.464) but 6.39 false events/minute versus 3.30 for Kinetic-TopoGuard.

\subsection{Auditable feature contribution}

We use \emph{auditable post-hoc decomposition}, rather than claiming intrinsic model
interpretability. Models and operating thresholds are frozen before the held-out test
runs are explained. Grouped permutation moves complete equal-length run blocks, preserving
within-run temporal dependence; TreeSHAP is an optional secondary view of the selected
tree components \cite{fisher2019all,lundberg2020local}. Neither explanation is used for feature, threshold, calibration, or model
selection.

The detailed Supplementary Material S1 table reports the change in event $F_1$ after each
group is permuted. Graph summaries are the highest-ranked group for both Kinetic-TopoGuard
(mean drop 0.196, 95\% CI [0.158, 0.244]) and Source-Gated Kinetic-TopoGuard
(0.096 [0.055, 0.151]). Persistence-image bins contribute to Kinetic-TopoGuard
(0.062 [0.012, 0.111]) but not detectably to the source-gated model
($-0.0004$ [$-0.0217$, 0.0178]). Projected-fragmentation statistics have smaller
positive contributions in both models, whereas speed summaries and most pair-distance
subgroups have intervals spanning zero. For component-count MAE, all Kinetic-TopoGuard
grouped importances are zero in these five seeds; the frozen count prediction is therefore
not presented as feature-driven. Source-gated count effects are dominated by current state,
and pair-distance deltas show a small negative contribution. Negative and unsupported
effects are retained in the machine-readable outputs and Fig.~\ref{fig:explainability}.

\subsection{Horizon and feature-source sensitivity}

The residual audit directly tests the count branch against its persistence anchor. The
validation-selected scale is zero in 19 of 20 seeds (95\%). Across held-out tests, selected
minus persistence MAE is $+0.000650$ with a seed-bootstrap 95\% CI [0, 0.001949] and
Wilcoxon $p=0.317$. No seed improves, 19 are unchanged, and one worsens. Count forecasting
is therefore retained as an audited component, not claimed as a primary contribution.

The Supplementary Material S1 horizon table prevents the \SI{600}{ms} result from being
interpreted as universal. Longer horizons change both class prevalence and the set of
alerts that can match a transition, so event recall and event $F_1$ are reported alongside
MAE and sample $F_1$.

\subsection{Real-data transfer and scope}

The forestry transfer tests whether a model trained on simulated mobility remains bounded
on measured trajectories. It does not test measured radio. Raw and physically clipped
predictions are both archived so that clipping cannot silently create the reported error.
The counterfactual graphs contain five, two, and four connected-to-fragmented transitions
at the three radii, respectively. All three models obtain zero event recall and event
$F_1$ under their frozen thresholds. Low count MAE and high fragmented-sample $F_1$ must
therefore not be read as successful early warning on this trace. Detailed per-radius
results are retained in Supplementary Material S1.

The AERPAW LTE result is strong under chronological holdout: $F_1$ rises from 0.828 to
0.961 on Dataset 22 and from 0.679 to 0.980 on Dataset 23. Throughput is different. The
random forest reduces MAE on the final-30\% split but produces a strongly negative $R^2$;
on the first-half-to-second-half split it is worse than the training-mean baseline.
Together with lag-1 autocorrelation above 0.98, this is negative evidence against the
earlier optimistic throughput claim. AERPAW therefore supports cellular link-state
transfer, not robust throughput prediction or inter-UAV FANET validation. Split-specific
metrics are supplied in Supplementary Material S1.

The measured \SI{60}{GHz} logistic model improves held-out long-distance link $F_1$ from
0.731 to 0.923 and PR-AUC to 0.985. The forestry topology values in Supplementary Material
S1 are reported only as transported sensitivities. Primary rows remain within measured
distance support and use the same SNR-plus-received-power definition as the held-out link
test.

MILUV provides simultaneous measured motion and inter-robot UWB topology for three flying
vehicles, but only one compatible sequence and one or two connected-to-fragmented events
per threshold. It is therefore used as a sparse cross-domain stress test rather than
strong external validation. The original whole-sequence frozen sensitivity in
Supplementary Material S1 is retained as the primary zero-shot artefact and is not mixed with
adaptation. At the primary \SI{-90}{dBm} threshold, Kinetic-TopoGuard has count MAE 0.045
but event $F_1=0.08$; shallow machine learning reaches event $F_1=0.40$ while its count
MAE exceeds 1.28. These are measured UWB topology-event outcomes, not outdoor range,
packet delivery, or routing evidence.

The secondary chronological study, detailed in Supplementary Material S1, uses an early prefix,
a guard interval, and an independent later test segment from the one compatible sequence.
Zero-shot frozen, chronological calibration, and few-shot prediction-head rows remain
separately labelled. At the same primary threshold, every model and all three protocols
obtain event $F_1=0$. Calibration improves some Brier scores but provides no event-$F_1$
benefit; this is a negative transfer result, not an equivalence conclusion. A
leave-one-scenario-out result is unavailable rather than synthesized from a single sequence.
The repeated five-seed fits quantify algorithmic variability on this fixed trace; they do
not increase the number of independent measured scenarios or fragmentation events.

\subsection{Operating points, packet behaviour, and latency}

Operating points are selected per seed on validation event $F_1$ subject to one or two
false event alerts per minute, then applied once to test runs. If a budget has no feasible
threshold, the exported constraint flag records that fact; the analysis does not replace
the policy with a test-selected threshold. Both budgets are feasible in all 20 validation
seeds. On test runs, the two-event policy attains event $F_1=0.421$ at 1.85 false
events/minute; the one-event policy trades recall for precision, yielding event
$F_1=0.351$ at 0.90 false events/minute. The complete operating-point table is in
Supplementary Material S1.

The one-step packet-load sweep, detailed in Supplementary Material S1, separates graph
reachability from packet performance. At high offered load, contention-deadline drops dominate and all models
degrade. Kinetic-TopoGuard does not consistently outperform the shallow or persistence
controller. The correct conclusion is that better warning scores do not automatically
translate into better PDR under a constrained MAC and one-step control policy.

\section{Engineering implications and deployment boundaries}

The supported engineering decision is narrow: given an observed graph history, a frozen
model can rank short-horizon fragmentation risk and issue a warning at an operating point
selected on validation runs. The benchmark can then compare whether a simulated, bounded
relay response is preferable to no intervention or to a reactive rule. It does not support
autonomous flight control, route certification, field packet-delivery guarantees, transmit-
power authorization, or safe separation decisions. In particular, none of the 360 paired
closed-loop comparisons meets the predeclared joint packet-delivery and action-cost rule.

Computation is similarly model-specific. On the measured host at 30 UAVs, default-thread
P95 topology-to-decision latency ranges from \SI{7.82}{ms} for FANET-TopoGNN to
\SI{90.81}{ms} for Source-Gated Kinetic-TopoGuard. The serialized Source-Gated artefact is
27,021,053 bytes, compared with 191,061 bytes for Current-state ExtraTrees and 121,272 bytes
for Kinetic-TopoGuard. These local, trusted-artifact measurements establish neither a
Jetson benchmark nor a sensor-to-actuator deadline. Edge deployment would additionally
require profiling the chosen airframe computer, radio and telemetry ingestion, scheduling,
power draw, failsafe communication, and actuator response.

Field validation requires synchronized multi-UAV motion, inter-UAV radio, medium-access,
routing, packet, onboard timing, energy, and actuation observations under a preregistered
intervention protocol. The included interactive interface is only a replay of generated
and transported evidence; it is not part of the engineering-validation claim.

\section{Discussion}

The central result is not that more sources are always harmful, but that representation
richness and engineering utility are different objectives. Current-state ExtraTrees is
strong because the short forecast horizon, component history, component change, node count,
and active radius already encode much of the immediately relevant state. Adding correlated
or noisy summaries increases the ways in which validation-selected probability quality can
diverge from one-to-one event precision and false-alert burden. The locked and factorial
results show that better calibration alone is insufficient evidence of a better warning.

Feature contributions are conditional rather than universal. Run-block permutation assigns
a positive event contribution to graph summaries in both audited models, while persistence-
image and speed intervals for the Source-Gated model include zero. Topological and motion
descriptors may be useful when multi-scale structure or projected link closure is not
recoverable from recent state, but the present seeds do not support that generalization.
The residual audit gives the same warning for count regression: validation usually selects
the physical persistence anchor, so the branch is an audited fallback rather than a central
forecasting advance.

The measured-data layers also explain why simulation ranking cannot be transferred by
assertion. Cellular connection state, peer-link viability, field motion, and indoor UWB
topology observe different parts of the causal chain. Chronological MILUV calibration
improves probability errors without recovering event detection, which motivates explicit
domain calibration while showing that calibration cannot manufacture missing event signal.
Likewise, closed-loop packet outcomes depend on traffic, queues, deadlines, action delay,
and movement cost, not only warning $F_1$. Retaining these disagreements and negative results
turns the package into a falsifiable benchmark for future methods rather than a selected
demonstration of one model.

\section{Limitations}

No available dataset jointly provides synchronized outdoor multi-UAV motion, inter-UAV
radio frequency measurements, medium-access and routing state, packet delivery, onboard
timing, and physical actuation. The primary training domain is therefore simulated, and a
simulation-to-field gap remains despite temporally correlated radio errors and per-run
hardware offsets. The real-data sources contain small UAV counts and partial modalities:
MILUV has three indoor robots with UWB ranging quality, forestry supplies motion without
radio labels, AERPAW is UAV-to-infrastructure cellular, and WiNES supplies peer-link radio
without a matching network controller.

The reported latency is a host-process loop, not sensor-to-autopilot or end-to-end vehicle
latency, and no field deployment or embedded-hardware benchmark was performed. The radio
model abstracts shadowing, fading, and correlation; the traffic model uses a simplified
single collision domain, routing, queues, retransmission outcomes, and deadlines. The
stateful relay remains a simulated intervention with fixed speed, acceleration, reaction,
duration, and link-budget constraints. Consequently, no result establishes outdoor IP
packet delivery, energy consumption, regulatory feasibility, multi-channel interference,
safe autonomous actuation, or deployment readiness.

\section{Reproducibility}

The anonymous package contains configurations, source code, per-seed outputs, protocols,
derived real-data traces, hashes, generated tables, and figures. The MILUV downloader
records the official dataset DOI, archive identifier, archive MD5, member CRC values, and
per-file SHA-256 values. A readiness audit checks cache version, seed coverage, PyTorch
backend provenance, event columns, validation/test separation, horizon and factorial
grids, packet loads, latency stages, support-aware \SI{60}{GHz} scope, MILUV transfer,
PDF page counts, anonymization, ZIP CRC, and package freshness.

The included interactive evidence replay binds only to generated CSV/JSON outputs and
supports operating-policy comparison. It is a reproducibility aid rather than an
independent experiment or hardware-connected system.

\section{Conclusion}

This study establishes a reproducible benchmark and cross-domain stress test for
fragmentation-event forecasting in unmanned aerial vehicle networks. Under the locked
primary test, Source-Gated Kinetic-TopoGuard does not show supported event-$F_1$ superiority
over Current-state ExtraTrees and produces more false alerts. The equal-learner factorial,
residual audit, measured-topology transfer, and closed-loop packet study independently show
that richer features, better calibration, or higher warning scores do not by themselves
establish engineering benefit. The supported contribution is therefore the leakage-
controlled evaluation framework, complete baseline accounting, explicit claim boundaries,
and reproducible negative evidence. Outdoor synchronized radio, packet, onboard-timing,
and actuation validation remains future work rather than a deployment result.
